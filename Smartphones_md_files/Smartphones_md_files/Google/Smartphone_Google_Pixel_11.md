## Technical Context & Operational Architecture

The Google Pixel 11 is built around Google Tensor G6 + Titan M3. Its heterogeneous compute design divides conventional application processing, graphics, machine-learning acceleration and security functions across purpose-built blocks. In practical use, this allows the Google Pixel 11 to perform Android application workloads while also running computational photography, voice processing, AI features and hardware-backed security without treating every workload as an identical CPU task.

The display subsystem of the Google Pixel 11 uses 6.3-inch Actua OLED, 1080×2424, 422 PPI, 60–120 Hz. Variable-refresh control changes panel timing according to interaction, while OLED pixel control provides deep blacks and high contrast. The Google Pixel 11 manages sustained heat through a distributed thermal-spreading approach tied to firmware power limits. Consequently, short bursts of compute can be substantially faster than long-duration workloads, and sustained gaming should be judged after the device reaches thermal equilibrium rather than from a launch benchmark.

The imaging architecture of the Google Pixel 11 combines 48 MP wide + 13 MP ultrawide + 10.8 MP 5x telephoto, up to 30x Super Zoom with autofocus, exposure, stabilization, sensor fusion and Google's computational photography pipeline. The Google Pixel 11 chassis uses Gorilla Glass Victus 2, polished back, spacecraft-grade aluminium, and IP68 resistance is intended to reduce exposure risk from dust and water under Google's specified test conditions. Camera processing, modem activity, display brightness and SoC load all contribute to heat and battery consumption, so the Google Pixel 11 should be evaluated as an integrated mobile system rather than as isolated components.

- The Google Pixel 11 uses software battery management and charging optimization to reduce unnecessary high-state-of-charge time, although lithium-ion battery capacity will naturally decline with age and charge cycles.
